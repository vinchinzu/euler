<p>A string forms an <i>addition equation</i> if it consists of</p>
<ul>
<li>Digits, forming positive integers. No leading zeros are allowed.</li>
<li>A single plus sign between integers.</li>
<li>A single equality sign.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most importantly, the equality must hold.</p>


<p>Example of strings of length 7 forming addition equations:
<br><code>1+1+1=3</code>
<br><code>100=100</code>
<br><code>77=7+70</code>
<br><code>1+2=2+1</code>
</p>

<p>Note that strings are considered different even if they form equivalent equations, so for example here are three unique strings:
<br><code>1+2=3</code>
<br><code>2+1=3</code>
<br><code>3=1+2</code>
</p>

<p>The following strings do not form valid addition equations: 
<br><code>1+1=3</code>
<br><code>1+1=02</code>
<br><code>0+1=1</code>
<br><code>+1=1</code>
<br><code>2-1=1</code>
</p>

<p>Let $A(n)$ be the number of strings of length not larger than $n$ forming addition equations.</p>

<p>You are given $A(3) = 9$, $A(5)=171$ and $A(7)=4878$.</p>

<p>Find $A(50)$. Give your answer modulo $10^9 + 7$.</p>
